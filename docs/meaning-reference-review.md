# Bounded bilingual meaning reference

## Initial claims

The initial candidate is `meaning_language.py`, version
`bennett-bilingual-meaning/1`. All German/English wording was newly composed for
development, not copied from a modern translation. Latin facts use the retained
1918 Bennett *New Latin Grammar*, HTML SHA256
`13f80dd1202e958854117c9c9cb2914630e92e37de2967791a45237f571327e6`,
including the original Gutenberg terms. No new acquisition was made.

- §§20, 23, 63, 101 supply gate/garden/boy/good/love and finite inflection tables.
  German development equivalents are Tor/Garten/Junge/gut/lieben, with stable
  English sense keys, not a newly inferred sense from a supplied gloss.
- The six present indicative active love cells preserve person and number.
  The initial `feel love` / `Liebe empfinden` alternatives propose the same
  isolated, object-unspecified predicate; independent review must judge whether
  these are defensible equivalents rather than merely compatible statements.
- §§141–143 support cum + ablative accompaniment: *cum portā*, with a gate.
  This is a literal constructed fragment, not a naturalness claim.
- §195 supports *porta hortī*, the gate of the garden. The kind of genitive
  relation is deliberately unspecified; possession, purpose and material are not
  interchangeable meanings. A definite reference is a controlled rendering,
  not a claim that Latin has a definite article.
- §234 supports *hortus bonus*, a good garden; §§170, 172, 174–175, 254 support
  *puer portam amat*, the boy loves the gate. Active/passive target-language
  paraphrases preserve the Latin argument roles, not Latin passive morphology.
- Explanations and role clauses have separate whole-utterance propositions.
  Appending a causal claim or naturalness rating is outside this vocabulary.
  Bounded-interpretation caveats disclaim exhaustivity; they do not certify it.
- No source supports general free-prose semantics, arbitrary contexts, additional
  senses, passive Latin, omitted material readings, curated cards or mnemonics.

## Independent dispositions

Independent read-only Latin/German/English review inspected the retained edition
and candidate bytes (`meaning_language.py` initial SHA256
`afbd80b3ac9ab262b64c5f9cd7641155eaa7e23388e6c528397cba2965a9362c`).
This is agent review of development propositions, not maintainer/human approval.

- Accepted the five lexical commitments and six primary present-active forms in
  each language. German words are composed equivalents, not Bennett quotations.
- Rejected all twelve `feel love` / `Liebe empfinden` candidates as established
  equivalents. Loving may express an enduring relation without asserting a
  currently experienced feeling. These can be compatible, not interchangeable.
  Runtime tuples now exclude them. §174a supports absolute use, not equivalence
  of every isolated finite cell with an experiential paraphrase.
- Accepted all four phrase pairs in both languages under the declared constructed
  readings. Added §222 for accompaniment, including Bennett's *cum febrī*.
  No attestation or naturalness of *cum portā* is asserted.
- Genitive wording preserves an unspecified relation, not an exhaustive list of
  contextual readings. Definite/indefinite Latin renderings need context (§20.1).
  This vocabulary covers the stipulated definite reading only; it cannot prove
  arbitrary references or exclusive interpretation of an isolated phrase.
- Adjective paraphrases preserve the selected evaluative sense. Active/passive
  English/German paraphrases preserve lover/loved roles; explanations refer to
  the Latin source's active voice. No Latin passive table is inferred.
- Accepted the listed explanations and role clauses subject to those bounds.
  The bounded caveat disclaims exhaustivity; it does not discharge an omitted
  material reading. No appended causal or naturalness claim is supported.

The review read source sections through the digest-checking accessor and checked
edition front matter. It performed no acquisition, hosted inference or edits.
Runtime enforcement and complete proof reuse require separate implementation QA.

## Correction candidates after independent runtime review

The initial complete-phrase claim did not establish the context its reference
wording assumed. Independent review and challenge upheld this gap: Latin has no
article (§20.1), and §222 supplies accompaniment with a motion predicate. An
internal statement that a reading was stipulated did not bind that premise to
the exercise, learner or blind requests. The original diagnostic positives remain
execution evidence, not proof that these applicability obligations were satisfied.

Version `bennett-bilingual-meaning/2` proposes explicit exercise-context evidence
within the existing reference schema, rather than changing versioned keys. Only
the exact reviewed contexts can qualify the finite phrase vocabulary. The
`constructed-exercise-context` tag is an opt-in display/disclosure convention,
not source authority. Missing, changed or additional context keeps the card partial.

The proposed context wording is retained in `meaning_language.CONTEXTS`:

- Accompaniment: a hypothetical setting in which someone has set out, including
  Bennett's `profectus est …` frame, with indefinite noun reference. The wording
  does not reveal the target noun's case, the accompaniment analysis or its
  translation. §222 supplies the motion-frame premise; §142 supplies government.
- Genitive and verb clause: use definite reference for both nouns. This is an
  explicit exercise premise, not an assertion that Latin has definite articles.
- Adjective phrase: use indefinite reference for the noun. No additional
  adjective sense or contextual naturalness is inferred.

The twelve experiential utterances remain rejected as equivalents. They are
proposed as known compatible-but-unestablished diagnostics only, never positives.
Other recognized finite love cells can conflict with the selected source's
person/number. English `you love` is not treated as a number contradiction against
second-person plural, because that wording permits plural reference. Mixed
explicit alternatives containing an equivalent and a known conflicting reading
receive an ambiguous-alternatives diagnostic, not equivalence.

Two additional negative utterances are proposed for independent semantic review:
`the gate loves the boy` and `das Tor liebt den Jungen`. They reverse the selected
Latin lover/loved roles. A source-relative contradiction diagnostic means that
role/person/number mapping conflicts with the translation task; it does not mean
that both described events could never occur. Arbitrary new wording remains
uninterpreted. These are bounded diagnostics, not a general contradiction parser.

These correction candidates were initially pending independent dispositions.
The fresh Latin/German/English source-only reviewer accepted the exact context
wording and finite diagnostics in the targeted correction follow-up:

- The definite/indefinite instructions supply an exercise restriction, not Latin
  articles or a uniquely identified real referent (§20.1).
- The hypothetical departure setting supplies §222's motion premise for the
  bounded accompaniment fragment. It does not attest the complete gate phrase
  or establish naturalness, idiom or exhaustive uses of `cum`.
- All twelve experiential candidates are compatible but not established as
  interchangeable isolated-form references. This is not a claim that English
  simple-present `feel` must denote a momentary feeling or that contextual
  equivalence is impossible.
- The two reversed-role sentences conflict with the selected translation mapping,
  not with the logical possibility of both events. Other finite person/number
  cells conflict under the same task reference frame; unmarked English `you love`
  remains unresolved for second-person plural. Exact lowercase German `sie lieben`
  is third plural; polite `Sie lieben` and case-folded variants are not interpreted.
- Mixed equivalent/conflicting alternatives diagnose the submitted answer, not
  ambiguity in the Latin source.

The reviewer checked retained Bennett §§20, 101, 142, 170, 172, 174, 195 and 222
against the correction candidate; all thirteen candidate hashes and the retained
HTML digest matched before and after review. Separate Python, proof-integrity and
claims reviewers verified enforcement, disclosure and finite/general scope.
The coordinator reconciled the original L1/L2/C02/C01 findings as fixed within
these bounds, preserving the original failed candidate and review history.
This is independent agent review of newly composed development wording, not human
