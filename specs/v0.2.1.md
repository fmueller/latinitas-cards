# LatinitasCards v0.2.1

## Summary

Planned, inactive release. Adds deck-based phrase and grammar practice after v0.2.0's
form analysis and managed updates. Recombine reviewed vocabulary already in the learner's
Anki deck into short, natural Latin combinations, with translation and grammatical
recognition exercises. This is a bounded deck-first feature, not corpus-first generation
or a prerequisite for the v0.3.0 corpus model.

## Goals

- Practise familiar vocabulary in meaningful combinations rather than only in isolation.
- Recognize cases, numbers, agreement, and conjugated verb forms in short phrases.
- Connect grammatical form to meaning without teaching ambiguous forms as unambiguous.
- Reuse existing deck profiles, stable identities, previews, and file-export workflows.
- Let humans and agents confirm or correct every generated linguistic claim from the CLI.

## Potential Features

### Deck-Based Phrase And Grammar Practice

Select reviewed lexical entries and senses from an existing deck through its confirmed
profile. Use a bounded set of reviewed construction patterns to produce preposition–noun,
noun–genitive, verb–noun, and adjective–noun combinations, including short clauses where a
conjugated verb needs them. Vary supported noun cases and numbers, adjective agreement,
and verb person, number, and tense only where the lexical data and pattern support them.
Missing paradigms, uncertain senses, unsupported forms, and unsuitable combinations are
reported rather than silently guessed. Content vocabulary comes from selected deck
entries; any pattern-supplied function words are explicit in preview.

Patterns must capture case government, agreement, and relevant verb sense and valency,
not merely concatenate inflected words. A combination must be both grammatically valid
and meaningful for its selected senses. Start with reviewed, limited patterns rather
than claiming that every pair of deck words can be combined naturally. General automatic
syntax analysis remains in v0.5.0.

Illustrative combinations, when the corresponding entries are available:

| Latin | Meaning | Grammatical focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cum amico` | with a friend | `cum` governs the ablative; `amico` is ablative singular here |
| `ad amicum` | to/towards a friend | `ad` governs the accusative; `amicum` is accusative singular |
| `liber amici` | the friend's book | `amici` is genitive singular in the intended reading |
| `puella amicum videt` | the girl sees a friend | nominative subject, accusative object, third-person singular present verb |

Acceptance: reviewed synthetic deck entries generate supported combinations across
contrasting cases and verb forms. Fixtures distinguish correct case government and
agreement from a plausible but incorrect word combination, and reject unsupported forms
or incompatible senses. A learner can select vocabulary and patterns without supplying
a corpus or running an LLM.

### Translation And Form Recognition Recipes

Offer separate, selectable card recipes with one primary learning objective each:

- Latin combination to a reference translation, followed by self-assessment rather than
  exact German or English string grading.
- Latin combination to an analysis of designated forms and their contribution to the
  construction, including applicable case, number, gender, person, tense, mood, or voice.

Answers include a concise explanation of why the forms fit the combination. Translation
is available as supporting context for recognition cards. Supported inflectional variants
reuse familiar vocabulary but remain distinct exercises when the tested form or meaning
changes. Do not automatically generate every possible variant or both recipes for every
combination; save the user's vocabulary, pattern, and recipe selections and generation
limits for deterministic reruns.

Retain legitimate alternate analyses or translations. For example, `amico` in isolation
can be dative or ablative, while `cum amico` supplies evidence for ablative. `amici` can
also be nominative plural; an intended genitive reading of `liber amici` must not erase
other grammatical readings where the short context permits them. Withhold a recognition
exercise when the intended distinction cannot be taught honestly from its prompt.

Use reviewed reference content in the project's configured target language. German is
the initial/default language; English generated-content support remains a v0.4.0 feature.
The CLI control language remains English.

Acceptance: previews show complete prompts, answers, translations, explanations, and
alternate analyses where relevant. Tests contrast isolated ambiguous forms with
disambiguating combinations, and preserve ambiguity when context is insufficient.
Recipe selection and limits produce reproducible output without exact-string grading.

### Review, Identity And Export

Reuse v0.2.0's linguistic review gates and managed-update contracts. Patterns and lexical
forms need reviewed evidence; newly generated combinations and their rendered answers
require explicit approval for grammatical correctness and naturalness before export.
Unchanged approvals can be reused, but changes to the phrase, analysis, or translation
require renewed approval. Preview includes contributing deck-entry identities, selected
senses, pattern identity, form choices, and review state.

A phrase exercise is a separate coherent learning object linked to its source entries,
not a merger of those entries or a new card attached arbitrarily to one word. Persist a
stable object key for the selected lexical identities/senses, construction, and tested
form choices. Recipe and semantic exercise keys distinguish translation and recognition
cards under the existing note/card identity contracts. Wording, gloss, or theme corrections
do not by themselves reassign identity; changes to the underlying exercise require review.

Export approved selections through the deterministic Anki CSV workflow. Preserve source
notes and user-owned fields; updating or withdrawing previously exported exercises uses
managed updates rather than assuming that omission from CSV removes a note in Anki.

Acceptance: reruns preserve identities and approvals for unchanged exercises, changed
answers require renewed review, distinct tested forms do not collide, and source lexical
notes remain separate and unchanged. Export excludes unapproved candidates.

### Reviewed Claim Decisions For All Generated Cards

Give humans and agents one CLI route to confirm, correct, or withhold every linguistic
claim that can appear on a generated card, so reviewed content reaches the export
without the Python API. This covers principal-part role labels (including PPP versus
supine), segmentations and explanations for both principal-part recipes, contextual
form-parsing features, and the phrase and recognition recipes above. It applies to every
confirmed profile and every recipe that renders reviewed claims; a recipe that
cannot consume the shared decisions is not complete.

List open claims per source, profile and recipe with candidate identity, form, proposed
value (if any), alternatives, evidence and current status. A decision records the claim
fingerprint, `accepted` or `withheld`, a reviewer and a reason. A reviewer may also author
or correct the value, for example a split or a German explanation; the authored value
becomes a new claim bound to its own evidence and review, not an edit to the proposal.
Store decisions in a versioned, human-readable sidecar beside the source or profile,
separate from destination state. Preview and export read the sidecar automatically and
report stale or unmatched decisions. Commands must work non-interactively with
machine-readable input and output so an agent can review in batches. Interactive
prompting is optional.

The existing gates still apply. Accept only individually reviewed claims: no command
approves all open claims at once or treats analyzer, LLM or agent confidence as review.
Changing the form, context, evidence, alternatives or linguistic profile invalidates a
decision; presentation changes do not. A decision is an attestation, not
authentication, export approval or managed-plan approval. Absent forms remain absent;
a reviewer may supply a missing form only as a separately reviewed source correction.

Pair the decision commands with read-only inspection, so a human or an agent can check
generated data in a loop without starting Anki. Given a source/profile with its sidecar,
an exported CSV, or an `.apkg`/`.colpkg`, show per note and card the LatinitasID, recipe
and slot, eligibility, the rendered prompt and answer as plain text, and every claim
behind them with status, fingerprint and evidence. Explain why each withheld or absent
role or feature is missing. A check mode compares an exported CSV or package with the
current source, profile and sidecar. It reports unreviewed asserted content, stale
decisions, drifted fields, and identity or slot mismatches, and exits nonzero on any
problem. Inspection never writes files, reviews or destination state, and needs no
network, LLM or Anki installation.

Acceptance: for each supported recipe and profile, a CLI-recorded acceptance makes the
reviewed split, explanation, label or feature appear on exported cards. A withheld or
missing decision keeps it off the card. An authored correction replaces the proposal
only after review. Stale decisions are reported and ignored. The same sidecar produces
identical CSV across reruns. Inspection shows every card's rendered text and its
claims as text and JSON. Its check mode passes on a fresh export and fails on a
tampered, stale or unreviewed one, all without Anki. Tests cover agent-style JSON
batches, conflicting decisions, profile changes and unchanged note/card identities.

### Configurable Grammatical Terminology

Make the language of grammatical terminology on generated cards a versioned profile
setting with an effective-override report. It covers feature names (case, number,
person, tense, mood, voice, gender), feature values (for example nominative, plural,
perfect) and principal-part role labels. Labels are hard-coded today: form parsing uses
Latin terms (`Casus`, `Numerus`, `Vox`) and principal-part roles use German
(`Präsens, 1. Person Singular`).

Offer two choices: Latin terminology (`Casus: nominativus`, `Numerus: pluralis`) as the
default, and the profile's configured user language. German (`Kasus: Nominativ`,
`Numerus: Plural`) is the only user language for now; English follows with English
generated content in a later release. Prompts remain Latin-first. Explanations and other
prose stay in the user language whatever the terminology setting. Unknown settings fail
validation.

Localize values from a controlled vocabulary per feature rather than translating
free-text reviewer input. Reviewed claims record the canonical value; display mapping is
presentation and does not invalidate reviews. Values outside the vocabulary need review
and are not silently translated. A terminology change alters generated answer fields only,
never note/card identity, slots or claims. Applying it to an existing destination needs
the usual renewed payload approval.

Acceptance: both settings render the expected terms for every feature and role, for
principal-part comparison and form-parsing cards. Switching settings keeps claim
fingerprints, review status and note/card identities unchanged. Latin is the default for
new and upgraded profiles. Preview reports the effective terminology. Unsupported
languages fail clearly.

### Reviewed Form Translations

On the answer side of principal-part cards, show the user-language translation of each
present principal part next to the form. For example, in German: `amō` → *ich liebe*,
`amāre` → *lieben*, `amāvī` → *ich habe geliebt / ich liebte*, and a role-appropriate
rendering of the PPP (*geliebt*) or supine. Show it for the tested form and in the
four-role comparison. Never show it on the front, so prompts remain Latin-first and
recognition cards do not give the answer away.

Treat each translation as a reviewable claim for that form and role, bound to the
selected sense, under the same review gates and CLI decisions as labels, splits and
explanations. A candidate may be drafted from the entry's meaning and the role, by rules,
or by an optional analyzer or LLM, but it reaches a card only after review. Several
senses, deponents (passive form, active meaning), defective or irregular verbs, and an
unconfirmed PPP/supine role need explicit review or stay withheld. Absent and withheld
roles show no translation. Translations use the user language (German now, English
later) whatever the terminology setting. Adding or changing them changes generated
answer fields only, never note/card identity or slots.

Acceptance: reviewed translations appear on the answer for each tested form and in the
comparison, for both recipes. Unreviewed, ambiguous, absent or withheld ones are not
asserted, and fronts never show them. Fixtures cover a regular verb, a deponent, a
multi-sense entry and PPP versus supine.

### Automatic Light/Dark Appearance

Add `auto` to the profile's morphology `appearance` setting, next to `light` and
`dark`. With `auto`, generated markup carries no fixed appearance, and the reference CSS
follows the client's night mode: the classes Anki Desktop and AnkiMobile set in night
mode, falling back to the light palette elsewhere. Switching the Anki or iOS
appearance then recolours existing cards without regeneration or reimport. Fixed `light`
and `dark` stay available and unchanged. Bump the reference style version for the new
CSS. The CSV still installs no CSS, so the manual or carrier setup must apply it.

`auto` is presentation only. It changes no claims, note/card identity or slots, and
moving an existing deck to it needs the usual renewed payload approval and a reimport
as an update. Do not add JavaScript. If a client sets no night-mode class, the light
palette must stay fully readable.

Acceptance: profile validation accepts `auto`, and preview reports it. Rendered markup
and CSS give the light palette by default and the dark palette under each client's
night-mode class. Tests cover monochrome and muted. Native checks on Desktop and
AnkiMobile confirm that toggling night mode recolours `auto` cards and keeps them
readable before this is called verified.

## Caution

- Morphological validity alone does not establish idiomatic or natural Latin.
- Short context cannot always distinguish case or syntactic interpretation.
- Deck entries may lack the sense, paradigm, or valency evidence a pattern needs.
- Combinatorial growth can overwhelm a deck; selection and limits are required.

## Recommendation About LLM Support

An optional, network-explicit agent may propose combinations, translations, or explanations
for review. Provider confidence is not evidence of grammatical correctness or naturalness.
The core CLI remains LLM-free, with deterministic reviewed patterns and explicit approval
before export; no provider or running model endpoint is required.

## Explicitly Excluded

- Corpus-first generation, corpus ingestion, or unrestricted sentence generation.
- Exhaustive declension/conjugation drills or coverage of every Latin construction.
- General automatic syntax-role analysis or syntax coloring.
- Arbitrary vocabulary bundles without a coherent construction and learning objective.
- German-to-Latin production, automatic translation grading, or English generated content.
- Direct integration with a running Anki instance or a required LLM service.
